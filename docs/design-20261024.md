# The Cooling Tower: design and plant research (2026-10-24)

Draft design; not built. Astra's idea (2026-10-24): a rave world in a nuclear plant cooling tower, with a stage in the tower itself (coloured smoke, radioactive colours), a way down into the nuclear reactor, and the DJ on the reactor or in the control room, close to the dance floor. One or two DJ spots, not decided yet.

Her reference: a TikTok by `collide.` (2022-12-10, #nuclearpowerplant) walking into a cooling tower during a refuelling outage: the X-shaped legs at the base, the ladder cage up the outside of the shell, scaffold walkways, the grating deck inside ("Normally water vapors funnel through here"), and the round opening of sky above ("The echo is unreal in here").

## 1. What a real plant looks like

### The cooling tower (natural draft, hyperbolic)

From the ground up:

| Part | What it is | Typical size | In the world |
|---|---|---|---|
| Diagonal legs ("X-legs") | Pairs of slanted concrete columns holding the shell up; the gaps between them are the air inlet all the way round | 40–50 pairs, 8–12 m tall | The entrance and the outer ring; light and fog spill out between the legs |
| Cold water basin | A shallow concrete pond under the whole tower, with a deeper sump where pumps draw the cooled water | Basin 100–150 m across | A dark mirror under the deck; drained during an outage, so it can be a lower floor |
| Rain zone | Open space under the fill where the cooled water falls as rain into the basin | 8–12 m tall | Under-deck lights, falling particle "rain" |
| Fill | Packing (plastic film sheets or splash bars) the hot water trickles over while air rises through it | 1–2 m deep, covers the whole floor plan | The dance floor is the walkway grating on top of it |
| Hot water distribution | A big feed channel from the plant, then flumes and pipes with spray nozzles over the fill | Pipes 1–3 m | Railings, flumes and nozzles from the video; bass-reactive spray jets |
| Drift eliminators | Rows of blades over the sprays that catch droplets | Thin layer above the sprays | A ceiling of slats just above head height |
| Shell | The hyperbolic concrete chimney; air heats, rises and pulls more air in at the base (stack effect) | 120–200 m tall, wall only 0.2–0.25 m thick, thicker at the bottom | The giant echoing room; the throat narrows then widens to a ring of sky |
| Outside ladder and cage | Maintenance ladder up the outside shell, with platforms | Full height | A climbable (or teleport) ladder to a rim lookout |

Air goes in at the bottom through the legs, up through the fill and out the top. Hot water from the plant goes up a riser, sprays over the fill, cools and falls into the basin, and the pumps send it back. The white plume is water vapour, not radioactive. During a refuelling outage the tower is dry and walkable, as in the video.

### The rest of a pressurised water reactor (PWR) plant

A real plant is three loops that never mix:

1. **Primary loop (inside containment):** the reactor vessel heats pressurised water; it runs through 2–4 steam generators and reactor coolant pumps.
2. **Secondary loop (turbine building):** steam from the steam generators spins the turbine and generator, then is condensed back to water in the condenser.
3. **Circulating water (to the tower):** cooling water runs from the condenser to the cooling tower and back through huge pipes or tunnels, 3–4 m across, often underground.

Buildings on a typical site: the round **containment** (reactor building) with a domed roof and a **polar crane** that runs round the top; the **auxiliary** and **fuel buildings** beside it (spent fuel pool); the long **turbine hall**; the **control room** in its own hardened building, usually between containment and the turbine hall (two units sometimes share one control room); and the cooling towers, set apart from everything.

### The reactor during a refuelling outage (the "currently off" in the video)

- The vessel head is lifted off by the polar crane and set on a stand.
- The **refuelling cavity** above the vessel is flooded, about 7–8 m of water over the core, so workers stand on the bridge above it.
- A **refuelling machine** (a bridge with a mast) lifts fuel assemblies out underwater and sends them through a transfer tube to the spent fuel pool.
- The blue glow is **Cherenkov light**, from fast electrons in the water. It is strongest over freshly used fuel.

## 2. Reality vs. the world

What is real and what we change, so nobody is surprised:

- In a real plant you cannot walk from the cooling tower down to the reactor; they are hundreds of metres apart and only connected by water pipes. **World choice:** a **circulating water tunnel** leads from the tower basin to the plant. It is a real kind of structure, just made short.
- The reactor sits in containment, not under the tower. In the world, the tunnel ends in the **reactor hall** (containment), so it feels like "down into the reactor".
- The steam is water vapour, so coloured smoke in the tower is pure show. "Radioactive colours" (Cherenkov blue, uranium-glass green, warning yellow) are a style, not a real hazard.
- The DJ "on the reactor" can stand on the **refuelling bridge**, which really does run right over the open, glowing vessel.

```
              ring of sky (open top, plume particles)
                        |
          [ SHELL — huge echo room, smoke columns ]
                        |
   [ STAGE 1: tower deck — dance floor on fill grating ]   <- spawn walks in between the X-legs
     DJ option A: platform on the hot water riser in the middle
                        |
            stairs down past the rain zone
                        |
      [ dry basin -> circulating water tunnel (3–4 m pipe) ]
                        |
   [ STAGE 2: reactor hall — open vessel glowing blue ]
     DJ option B: on the refuelling bridge over the cavity
     DJ option C: control room with a window onto the floor
```

- **Stage 1, the tower:** the floor is the walkway grating over the fill, railings and flumes as in the video. Coloured smoke rises through the deck as if it were the steam. Lasers point up at the ring of sky.
- **Stage 2, the reactor hall:** a round, domed concrete hall with the polar crane overhead. The open vessel glows blue in the flooded cavity; the dance floor rings the cavity behind a railing.
- **DJ spots:** two booths, one live at a time (see Decisions): A in the tower, B on the refuelling bridge.

### Two new tools Astra proposed (2026-10-24), not built

Both would live in Unity Unicorn (findastra/unity-unicorn), next to the vine tool, so every later world can use them.

- **Pipe Spawner.** AI-made rails and pipes get the straight runs right but leave the corners broken. Pipe Spawner takes a path you draw (points in the scene) and builds straight runs plus real curved elbows (bend radius about 1.5× the pipe width), flanges and hangers at every turn. Railings have the same problem, so it should also build railings: posts at every corner, top rail and knee rail that meet, toe board. Later it could do ladder cages and cable trays. Decided: pipes and railings (Q5).
- **CAD Converter.** Brings real engineers' CAD files into Unity so we can pick the parts we like. Reality check:
  - Public CAD (GrabCAD, TraceParts, manufacturers' sites) is mostly *parts*: valves, pumps, flanges, cranes, grating, ladders. Complete nuclear plants are not published, for security.
  - SolidWorks and Fusion 360 files are exported to STEP; FreeCAD (free, not installed yet) turns STEP into meshes, then Blender slims them (CAD meshes are very heavy) and adds UVs and materials before Unity.
  - Each file's licence must allow reuse in a public world; record every file with its maker and licence.
  - The big shapes (the hyperbolic shell, X-legs, basin) are simple geometry and are faster to build from real dimensions than to find.
  - Decided: a parts library, not a whole-plant hunt (Q6).

Fog and smoke: VRChat has no built-in volumetric fog, so smoke is particle systems with soft-particle materials, plus VRSL beams and Unity distance fog.

Performance targets from VRChat's community guide: about 200 batches, under 350k triangles, under 100 MB. The shell is mostly one smooth surface of revolution, so it is cheap; the X-legs, grating and railings are the expensive parts and should use instancing or baked textures.

**Reopened 2026-10-24:** Astra asked for the DJ's place to carry meaning in how a plant works (the DJ runs the instruments, so should the sound come from the reactor?). Questions 1–3 are back open in a second deck, "what the DJ means" (8 questions: who the DJ is, where the sound comes from, what the dancers are, which way the night flows, what happens on the breakdown and drop, how much the world teaches, first tools, first water). The tools plan is in [infrastructure-and-tools-20261024.md](infrastructure-and-tools-20261024.md).

**New direction, later 2026-10-24 (Astra):** the world is entered like a break-in: a rave crew sneaks into the dry tower during a refuelling outage. Decided by Astra:

- The crowd is a crew in **hard hats with clipboards** (pick-up props, plus a hard-hat avatar to switch into).
- People **dance in the stack** (the cooling tower), and the lighting, smoke and lasers all go there.
- The DJ controls the lights, so the DJ must see the dance floor; monitors are fine.
- Every DJ plays from **their own stream link** on a third-party host; a **Light Show Generator** turns that stream into the light show with no delay, reusable for future DJ worlds.

Still open, in the "breaking in" deck (12 questions): where in the stack the crowd dances (recommended: the cleared centre of the drained basin, fill ring as balcony), the DJ's operating room (recommended: the outage crew's site office on scaffolding at the wall, window onto the floor), how the DJ watches (window + monitor wall of in-world cameras), what the reactor hall becomes (a secret room with the set piped in), the way in (a cut fence, under the X-legs), the tower's size (half size, about 75 m tall), how much the DJ steers the Light Show Generator, plus teaching, first tools, first water and the caretaker pet's owner and name.

Realism notes for the stack: fill normally covers the whole floor about 10 m up and cannot be stood on (only its walkways); during outages sections are pulled out for replacement, which is where the cleared centre comes from. A tower is a chimney, so real stage fog would rise in a column and pour out the top.
